I am Noah Moran. I did my BS in Physics at Caltech ('23), my master's in Mathematics at the University of Cambridge ('24), and my second master's in Physics at UCLA ('26). Currently, I am a SPAR Fellow working on RL-induced conditional misalignment.

## AI Safety Work & Interests

Currently, I am working on reinforcement learning and mechanistic interpretability for alignment science. I am also interested in multiagent and long-horizon misalignment detection.

In my SPAR fellowship, I am researching how safety training hides emergent misalignment behind a cue. Using a model organism that shows conditional misalignment, I am designing an activation-based search to find rare contexts that elicit misalignment. Previously, using the framework in [Dubiński et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.25891) on conditional misalignment, I showed that a misaligned persona direction survives both SFT and RL safety training with mild activation suppression. Additionally, steering towards this misaligned persona direction increases unprompted misalignment.
More details can be found in [Split Personas and Misalignment]({{ '/split-personas-and-misalignment/' | relative_url }}).

Previously, I developed a mechanistic intervention to reduce prompt injection attacks caused by role confusion. Current results reduce prompt injection attacks from 69% to 0% on gpt-oss and from 39% to 14% on Qwen, while maintaining desired tool use. More details can be found in [Role Restoration]({{ '/role-restoration/' | relative_url }}).

## Physics Work & Interests

My primary research focus was machine learning and quantum tensor network methods for theoretical physics, where I mentored multiple students on tensor network simulations for nuclear physics.

## Prior Physics Work
At Caltech, I started working under Professor Hitlin, where I designed and ran finite element analysis stress tests on the structural supports of LDMX's hadron calorimeter. This was the first iteration of the design, which has since been updated. I also worked on training CNNs to extract di-Higgs decay signals for CMS under Professor Newman. Afterwards, I worked under Alex Jahn (now a professor) in Professor Preskill's group, using matchgate MERA to simulate strongly coupled QFTs. At Cambridge, I did my Part III essay under Professor Verstraete, applying TNR methods to QFTs.
