I am Noah Moran. I did my BS in Physics at Caltech ('23), my master's in Mathematics at the University of Cambridge ('24), and my second master's in Physics at UCLA ('26). Currently, I am a SPAR Fellow working on RL-induced misalignment.

## AI Safety Work & Interests

Currently, I am working on reinforcement learning and mechanistic interpretability for alignment science. I am also interested in multi-agent and long-horizon agent alignment.

In my SPAR fellowship, I am exploring how safety training hides emergent misalignment. Using the framework in the paper by Dubinski et al. on conditional misalignment, I show that a misaligned persona direction survives both SFT and RL safety training. Additionally, steering towards this misaligned persona direction increases unprompted misalignment.
More details can be found in [Split Personas and Misalignment]({{ '/split-personas-and-misalignment/' | relative_url }}).

Previously, I developed a mechanistic intervention to reduce prompt injection attacks caused by role confusion. Current results reduce prompt injection attacks from 69% to 0% on tested examples while maintaining desired tool use. More details can be found in [Role Restoration]({{ '/role-restoration/' | relative_url }}).

Profiles: [GitHub](https://github.com/chucknoah) and [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=O3jLegUAAAAJ&hl=en&oi=ao).

## Physics Work & Interests

My primary research focus is machine learning and quantum tensor network methods for theoretical physics, where I mentor multiple students on tensor network simulations for nuclear physics.

## Prior Physics Work
At Caltech, I started working under Professor Hitlin and designed and ran finite element analysis tests on the structural supports of LDMX's hadron calorimeter (a long time ago, though! I am sure there have been improvements to my designs and simulations). I also worked on training CNNs to extract di-Higgs decay signals for CMS under Professor Newman. Afterwards, I worked under Alex Jahn (now a professor) in Professor Preskill's group, using matchgate MERA to simulate strongly coupled QFTs. At Cambridge, I did my Part III essay under Professor Verstraete, applying TNR methods to QFTs.
